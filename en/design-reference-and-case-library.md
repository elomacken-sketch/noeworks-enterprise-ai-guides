# How can design teams organise past drawings and project references for AI retrieval?

> **Direct Answer**: A design firm's most valuable work is often buried in project folders named only by year and client code. Rather than bulk-uploading terabytes of raw archives, start by indexing 15 to 20 representative projects around the conditions designers actually search for: sector, floor area, budget tier, site constraints, and verified construction details. This guide explains working methods, not a completed customer case.

## Index archives by project constraints rather than folder dates

Archiving solely by year and contract number makes retrieval impossible for anyone who was not on the original team. Tag each project card with functional sector, area range, cost per square metre, key physical constraints (such as low ceiling height, heritage facade rules, or awkward circulation), and whether the design was built as proposed.

## Link concept decks to as-built drawings, material schedules, and cost notes

Pitch decks often show idealised renders that were later value-engineered during construction. Pair presentation slides with final construction details, material schedules, supplier notes, and site photos so teams can see which details held up on site and which caused budget overruns.

## Retrieve comparable internal benchmarks when a new brief arrives

When a new client brief comes in, AI queries the internal library using the target area, budget, and program mix to surface three to five comparable past projects—summarising space allocation ratios, circulation options, material palettes, and common client questions so lead designers start from proven institutional experience.

## Enforce confidentiality, IP rights, and site-specific verification

Record client NDA restrictions, publication rights, and local code assumptions on every project record. Past references inform internal methodology and benchmarking; designers must still re-verify dimensions, structural loads, and local regulations for the new site.

## Common Questions

### Do we need to clean up ten years of project archives before starting?

No. Start with 15 to 20 high-value projects from the past two years that represent your core service lines. Once the tagging schema proves useful in live briefs, add new projects as part of standard project close-out.

### How should heavy CAD, BIM, or design source files be handled?

Store readable PDF drawing sets, material schedules, cost summaries, and a one-page project summary card for AI retrieval, linked directly to the exact server path and version of the native CAD or BIM files.

### How do we stop junior staff from copying outdated technical details into new projects?

Every retrieved reference card should explicitly list the original site assumptions and a mandatory checklist of items that must be re-verified by the project engineer or lead designer.
